About SPATIAL-HW
What is this?

SPATIAL-HW is an extension of the point-based CLI-HEWS heatwave dashboard. Instead of analysing a time series for a single station, it applies the Hobday et al. (2016) heatwave definition to every grid point of a gridded temperature dataset (NetCDF / GeoTIFF) that has been clipped to a study area defined by a shapefile of administrative boundaries.

Workflow
  • 01 · Data Upload & Preprocessing — load the temperature grid and the study-area boundary shapefile, optionally mask / gap-fill / smooth the field.
  • 02 · Heatwave Computations — press Detect Heatwaves to compute heatwave metrics per grid cell, Statistics to summarise the region, and Trends to estimate spatial trends (linear regression or Mann-Kendall) with significance.
  • 03 · Visualization — map each metric over the domain, inspect any single point's series, animate metric evolution through time, and view region-wide annual totals.
  • 04 · Comparative Analysis — compare two climatological periods or two separate datasets and map the differences.
Methods

Detection uses the marineHeatWaves Python module (Eric C. J. Oliver et al.) implementing the hierarchical classification in A hierarchical approach to defining marine heatwaves. The atmospheric adaptation follows Wemegah et al., Evidence of Heatwaves: Characteristics and Trends in Selected Ghanaian Cities.

Input Format

A 3D temperature field (time × latitude × longitude) with daily resolution, either as a NetCDF file, or a stack of single-band GeoTIFFs (one per day) packed in a ZIP. Longitude values may be 0–360 or −180–180; they are standardised to −180–180 internally.
